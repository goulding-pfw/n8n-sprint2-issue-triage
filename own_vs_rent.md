# Own vs Rent

  While working on the n8n portion of this sprint, I assumed it would beat out whatever Zapier could do handily. I found n8n to be very intuitive, customizable, and extremely pleasant to use. I was shocked to find out that Zapier simplified everything down to essentially just be a plug-n-play version of n8n. It was quicker, smoother, and Zapier did all of the setup for me. That being said, I think I would still prefer to use n8n, as I personally like having more control and customization options available to me. Now that I've stated my position on these two tools, let's break everything down.
  
  During the initial setup for the locally hosted n8n instance, I had to manually connect and setup the GitHub webhook forwarding myself, including having the webhook forwarding service running in a local terminal. Zapier just asked for my GitHub account, requested permissions, and did everything for me. I will say that a part of me is a little concerned over providing such power over my repository to a third party, but this is a non-issue for small prototype projects. If this was an enterprise workflow that needed to connect to my company's repository, this likely will not be allowed without some conversations.

  Now I want to talk about aspects of these tools that touch on the assumptions and views I declared myself to have earlier this semester. I stated that my primary motivators for whether a company should choose whether to own or rent were financial costs, issues of data ownership, and a few other concerns. I would like to state that, irrespective to n8n and Zapier, there is an additional layer of consideration that may push companies to use managed software solutions versus owning the tech stack themselves. This consideration is of liability when things go wrong, particularly in the realm of cybersecurity. If a company is fully owning the tech stack, they are also responsible for the security of the software and the data. In today's modern era of AI hacking, this can easily result in major financial losses for the company when breaches occur. With a managed solution, the onus is on the company who owns the software and is managing the platform for its customers. When a breach occurs due to that company's failings, it provides legal cover and grounds for compensation for the customers who pay for the service. In this case, Zapier would be responsible to its customers to keep everything secure. When things go wrong, Zapier owns the failure, and Zapier's customers have the ability to tell their own customers that it was a failing with that company. This can protect the brand, limit legal liability, and even allow for any legal issues to route through to Zapier. This is a very important consideration for companies that can not field the cybersecurity necessary to keep everything safe on their own (even my last company was pushed further into Microsoft's environment due to issues with cyberattacks on our servers, and that company just made hot tubs!)
